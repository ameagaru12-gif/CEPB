# Research basis, limitations, and validation plan

Version: CEPB 1.0.0. Literature checked: 2026-10-10. CEPB is a usable operational protocol, not an empirically calibrated scale. Publishing the package does not establish construct validity, equal intervals between 0–5 ratings, scientifically optimal weights, or an operating certification authority.

## 2. Unresolved validity questions

- **Content validity:** Do 96 items sufficiently cover the intended scope without duplicate judgments?
- **Rating reliability:** Do references, severity judgments, and 0–5 anchors produce consistent interpretations across people and languages?
- **Construct validity:** Are character continuity, dialogue success, and experienced presence distinguishable from liking, verbosity, and fluency?
- **Generalization:** Do items retain their meaning across unseen characters, CANON/BOUNDED/RST, media, environments, languages, and user populations?
- **Weight sensitivity:** How much do weights and item correlations affect rankings?
- **Longitudinal procedure:** Are gaps, maintenance, bridges, new scopes, and recovery judged consistently?

Short-term structural tests do not validate a longitudinal measure. O's minimum of 12 participants guarantees neither statistical power nor population representativeness. The 36-month clock is a procedural time condition, not a calibration experiment for the entire scale.

## 3. Validation program

1. **Review content:** Authors, developers, experts in cognition, dialogue and media evaluation, and users review overlap and omissions.
2. **Build boundary evidence:** Include success, partial success, clear misattribution, and missing evidence. Distinguish manufactured calibration examples from observations of real products.
3. **Annotate independently:** Humans initially see neither other scores nor LLM suggestions. Retain initial ratings and adjudication; distinguish agreement on states from agreement on scores.
4. **Pilot:** Separate languages, character modes, styles, and user experience. Check O question comprehension, reverse wording, repair questions, and threshold stability.
5. **Use held-out confirmation:** Check characters, tasks, histories, and participants unused during the pilot. If thresholds change after findings, preserve earlier results and evaluate under a changed version.
6. **Run controls:** Separate changes to worlds, experiences, readout, and agreement pressure. Trace actual inputs and later choices.
7. **Check longitudinal evidence:** Accumulate lineage/scope clocks, changes, gaps, repairs, current capabilities at the endpoint, and independent checks.
8. **Report by version:** Retain all planned results, including performance, disagreement, counterevidence, cost, missing data, threshold sensitivity, and exclusions, within permissible access limits.

Research changing standard thresholds must declare a `Custom` procedure version. A later improved scale must not be silently applied retrospectively to CEPB 1.0.0 results.

## 4. Causation, internal states, and function

A quote, changing internal variable, different wording, or self-report alone does not identify a causal contribution from experience. Controls require baseline state, intervention target, actual inputs, matched world conditions, adoption and delivery, and subsequent outcomes.

Distinguish complete planned controls that found no effect on the specified metric from cases unidentified because of missing evidence or inadequate detection scope. Reflect the former as a non-detection within the tested conditions in the anchors; retain the latter as `unknown` or missing. Non-detection does not prove absence of internal change. Maintenance or deferral can succeed when connected to experience, even without visible change.

Restrict metacognitive metrics to tasks with correctness labels. Do not indiscriminately label preference directions, fictional values, or permitted antagonistic performance as real-world errors. When using ECE, Brier scores, discrimination measures, or related metrics, report basic performance, difficulty, counts, missing data, bin choices, and model assumptions.

## 5. Rater, LLM, and participant biases

Do not show LLM suggestions or others' scores to initial independent human raters, and exclude human comparison scores from initial LLM annotations. Later adjudication is a separate record. Check reversed order, meaning-preserving short/long pairs, anonymized names and products, preference for a model's own output, and preferences for agreement or refusal.

Participants who experienced the interaction answer O. Avoid leading them through promotion, desired scores, or explanations of functional success; allow inability to answer and withdrawal. Record liking, fandom, language, prior use, devices, order, and carryover separately. Repeated responses or many turns in one history do not increase independent participant counts.

Clarify consent and purposes and collect only needed data. Record access limits, pseudonymization, and the distinction between public summaries and restricted audits. Do not publish private conversations or third-party material without permission.

## 6. Usability and future validation

Version 1.0.0 provides operational rules for consistent handling of fixed denominators, nulls, evidence bundles, independent checking, and clocks. “Not yet calibrated” does not mean the procedure is undefined. Conversely, passing structural checks does not establish character understanding or scientific validity of a psychological measure.

Shared items, refreshed held-out tasks, versioned research, and independent reassessment support long-term use. CEPB does not predict or guarantee validity for five future years. Until relevant validation is published, do not describe operational thresholds as scientific thresholds or Verified as an official certification scheme.
